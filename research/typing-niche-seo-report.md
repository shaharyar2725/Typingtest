**Research Date:** May 7, 2026
**Depth:** Deep (5 subagents + 1 gap-fill)
**Sources Consulted:** 30+

---

## Executive Summary

The typing test niche is dominated by a small number of entrenched incumbents with strong brand loyalty, but it has substantial long-tail and informational keyword opportunity for a new entrant. The primary head keywords ("typing test" at 3.5M/mo, "typing speed test" at 700K/mo) are High competition and effectively owned by Monkeytype (12.8M/mo traffic, DR 65) and Typing.com (10M+/mo, DR 75). However, the informational layer of this niche — "is 40 WPM good?", "average typing speed by profession", "how long does it take to learn touch typing?" — is thin and poorly served by the big tools, which focus on the interactive product rather than content depth.

The winning strategy for TypeFlow is **topical authority**: become the most comprehensive resource for typing-speed knowledge (statistics, benchmarks, profession requirements, improvement guides) while the interactive tool drives direct and repeat traffic. Content gaps around profession-specific requirements, percentile data, and productivity ROI are genuinely unoccupied and highly searchable.

## Competitor Analysis

### Monkeytype (monkeytype.com)
- **Traffic:** ~12.8M monthly visits
- **DR:** 65 | **Backlinks:** 36K from 4.1K referring domains
- **Traffic Mix:** 71% direct (brand/product-led), ~20% organic
- **Unique Angle:** Minimalist, open-source, highly customizable. Developer community driven.
- **Content Strategy:** Almost no blog or informational content — relies entirely on product quality and word of mouth.
- **Weakness:** Zero informational content depth. No "what is a good WPM" page. No profession guides. Purely a tool.

### Typing.com
- **Traffic:** ~10M+ monthly visits
- **DR:** 75
- **Unique Angle:** K-12 school curriculum. 38M+ student users. Teacher dashboard.
- **Content Strategy:** Standards-aligned educational content, gamification for kids.
- **Weakness:** Not optimized for adult/professional use cases or real-world benchmarks.

### 10FastFingers (10fastfingers.com)
- **Traffic:** ~5.8M monthly visits (up 41% recently)
- **DR:** 61 | **Organic Keywords:** 11.1K
- **Unique Angle:** Competition-focused, multiplayer, 40+ languages.
- **Weakness:** Blog is thin. Informational content is basic.

### Keybr (keybr.com)
- **Traffic:** ~3.4M monthly visits
- **DR:** 68
- **Rank:** #1 for "typing practice" in the US
- **Unique Angle:** Algorithmic muscle-memory training based on key frequency.
- **Weakness:** No real informational content. No profession benchmarks.

### TypeRacer
- **Traffic:** ~2.0M monthly visits
- **DR:** 62
- **Unique Angle:** Gamified racing with multiplayer. Literature/movie quotes.
- **Weakness:** No educational content whatsoever.

**Pattern:** Every major competitor focuses entirely on the interactive tool. None has built a meaningful content hub around typing speed statistics, profession benchmarks, or improvement guides. This is the gap TypeFlow should fill.

## Key Data Points & Statistics (Cite These in Content)

- **Global average typing speed:** 41.6 WPM (measured across 10.4 million tests)
- **Professional range:** 65–75 WPM
- **Legal/court reporting:** 80–95+ WPM
- **Time savings:** Knowledge workers can save ~2 hours/day going from 40→60 WPM
- **High-income correlation:** Earners over $100K/yr spend 87% of their workweek typing
- **Accuracy cost:** One error = ~2.5 seconds of cognitive reset. 90% accuracy = 30% lost output
- **Gen Z gap:** Declining touch typing skills due to mobile-first habits
- **Regional high:** Northern Europe averages 48 WPM (highest globally)
- **Mobile average:** 32 WPM on smartphone with predictive text
- **5-min vs 1-min drop:** Most typists see 5–15% WPM drop from 1-min to 5-min test
- **Learning timeline:** 2–4 weeks to reach functional touch typing (30–40 WPM); 2–4 months to 60–80 WPM
